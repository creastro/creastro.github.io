Coronagraph Lab

Planets are billions of times fainter than the stars they orbit and sit only a fraction of an arcsecond away. A coronagraph removes the starlight so that the planet can be seen, and a deformable mirror, steered by a wavefront sensor, cleans up what the optics leave behind. Follow the light through the instrument, try nine coronagraphs and seven wavefront sensors, and close the loop to dig a dark hole.

What to look for

  • Lyot plane. Open plane C with the classical Lyot coronagraph: the starlight that leaks past the spot gathers in a bright ring on the pupil edge, and the stop cuts it away. With the vortex the inside of the pupil goes completely dark.
  • Perfect nulls need perfect pupils. The vortex and four-quadrant masks cancel the star of a clear circular telescope almost completely. Add a central obstruction or segments and the null is lost; the APLC and APP are designed for any shape.
  • Inner working angle. Move a planet towards the star: the mask dims it below about 1 λ/D for the vortex of charge 2, 3 λ/D for a 3 λ/D Lyot spot. Higher vortex charges reject more aberrations but push the IWA outwards (Expert: compute the throughput).
  • Speckles. A few nanometres of wavefront error leave a speckle halo far above 10⁻⁸. Each spatial frequency of the error makes a pair of speckles at the matching separation.
  • Blind spots of the AO loop. The pupil-plane sensor flattens its own arm. Non-common-path errors stay; focal-plane sensing (pairwise probing, SCC, phase diversity) sees them.
  • Dark hole. EFC with pairwise probing digs a one-sided hole between 3 and 7 λ/D by 2–3 orders of magnitude in a few iterations; speckle nulling gets there more slowly.
  • Bandwidth. At 20 % bandwidth the band-limited and Lyot masks leak more light (their size is fixed in λ0/D) while the vortex, an achromatic phase in this model, keeps its null.
  • Limits of the model. Scalar Fraunhofer optics; monochromatic control at λ0; no polarisation, no time-varying turbulence, no Fresnel propagation between optics; noiseless wavefront sensors; a pixelated pupil edge that limits the phase-mask nulls near 10⁻⁸; 64 or 128 samples across the pupil. Interaction matrices and Jacobians come from the unaberrated model, as on real instruments.
